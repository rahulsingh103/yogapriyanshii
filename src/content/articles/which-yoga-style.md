There are many styles of yoga, and the names alone can be confusing. The good news is that there is no wrong place to start. Most styles share the same roots: postures, breathing and a moment of stillness. What changes is the pace, the focus and how much effort a class asks of you.

Here is a simple way to choose, followed by a short guide to each class Priyanshi teaches.

## Three questions to ask yourself

- What would you like from yoga? Calm, flexibility, strength, care for your back, or support in pregnancy each point towards different classes.
- How much yoga have you done? If you are new, a steady, well-explained class helps you learn the basics. If you already practise, you may enjoy something stronger or more structured.
- How much energy do you have? Some days call for a strong flow, others for slow, quiet holds. Many people enjoy a mix across the week.

## Steady foundations: Hatha and Chakra Flow

Hatha is a good first class for most people. Postures are held for a few breaths and explained clearly, so you have time to find your alignment and connect movement with breath. No experience is needed.

Chakra Flow is a moving meditation that pairs breath with a gentle sequence. It is beginner friendly and suits anyone who would like to leave feeling calmer and more rested.

## Flowing and strong: Vinyasa, Ashtanga and Barre

Vinyasa links one pose to the next with the breath. It builds strength and focus, and suits people with a few classes behind them who enjoy moving.

Ashtanga follows a set sequence, in the same order each time, with a steady breath. Because the sequence repeats, you can feel your practice grow from week to week. It suits people who practise regularly and like structure.

Barre combines strength work and deep stretching, with small, controlled movements. All levels are welcome, and it is a good choice if you would like strength and flexibility in one class.

## Slow and quiet: Yin, Mobility and Spine Healing

Yin Yoga uses long, quiet holds, mostly seated or lying down, that give your body time to slow down and let go. It pairs well with a stronger practice, and it is welcoming if you are new.

Mobility Yoga is gentle movement for your joints and everyday ease, whatever your experience.

Spine Healing is gentle work for back care and release, at a pace that suits you. If you have a back injury or pain, tell Priyanshi before class so she can guide you.

## Pregnancy and after birth: Prenatal & Postnatal

These gentle classes are for mums-to-be and for mums after birth who would like to practise at their own pace. Priyanshi is trained in prenatal yoga. Let her know that you are pregnant, or when you gave birth, before your first class.

## Still not sure?

Whichever style you choose, you can always change your mind. The best style is usually the one you enjoy enough to come back to, and your practice can grow and shift with you over time.
