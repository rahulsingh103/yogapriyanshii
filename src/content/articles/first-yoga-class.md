Walking into your first yoga class can feel like a big step. You might wonder whether you are flexible enough, whether everyone else will know what they are doing, or whether you will be the only one who has never tried it. Those thoughts are completely normal, and every teacher has heard them many times. You do not need any experience, and you do not need to touch your toes. A good class meets you where you are.

This guide walks you through what to bring, what usually happens in a class and how to look after yourself on the mat, so you can arrive feeling a little more at ease.

## What to wear and bring

Choose clothes you can move and stretch in, and that stay in place when you bend forward or lift your arms. Leggings or soft trousers and a fitted top work well. Yoga is usually practised barefoot, so there is no need for special shoes.

A few simple things make the first class easier:

- A bottle of water.
- Your own mat if you are going to an in-person class.
- A small towel if you tend to get warm.
- A light stomach. Most teachers suggest no heavy meal for about two hours before you practise.

Try to arrive, or log in, five to ten minutes early. It gives you time to settle, set up your space and say hello.

## Tell your teacher a little about you

Before class begins, let your teacher know about anything that might matter: an injury, a health condition, recent surgery or a pregnancy. You do not need to share more than you are comfortable with. A quick word helps the teacher suggest a variation for a pose, or point you to an option that suits you better that day.

## What usually happens in a class

Every style is a little different, but many classes follow a similar shape. There are often a few quiet minutes at the start to arrive and notice your breathing. Then comes a gentle warm-up, followed by a series of postures, standing, seated or lying down, linked with the breath. Many classes include some balance work and stretching, and some include simple breathing exercises.

Most classes end with a few minutes of rest lying on your back, often called relaxation or savasana. Lying still can feel strange at first, but many people find it becomes their favourite part.

The teacher will usually show or describe each pose and offer simpler and stronger versions. You are always welcome to take the simpler one, or to pause.

## Move at your own pace

Yoga is not a competition, with other people or with yourself. Some poses will feel easy and others will feel new. A stretch can bring a feeling of effort or mild discomfort, and that is normal. Sharp, pinching or shooting pain is different: it is a sign to ease off or come out of the pose. Keep breathing steadily. If you notice that you are holding your breath, that is often a hint to back off a little.

Rest whenever you need to. Sitting or lying down for a few breaths is part of the practice, not a failure, and a good teacher will never mind.

## After class

You may feel calm, a little tired or pleasantly loose. Drink some water and take a moment before you rush on with your day. If some muscles feel slightly tender the next day, that is common after trying a new kind of movement. If anything felt wrong, mention it to your teacher before your next class.

Then notice how you felt. Yoga tends to make more sense with a few classes behind you, so it is worth giving it more than one try before you decide.
